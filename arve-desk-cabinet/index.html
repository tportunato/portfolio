    How it works

    1. Closed, it is a 700 × 500 × 1250 mm box. The front is two wing boxes, 349 mm each, meeting at a centre seam.
    2. Each wing hangs on three butt hinges at the carcass's front corner, swings 160° and parks beside the carcass with its shelves facing you.
    3. The wing shelves overhang their box by 40 mm. When the wings close, that overhang tucks into a 50 mm recess in front of the flap.
    4. The flap hangs from two secretary hinges under the fixed shelf. Lifted 90°, it lands flush with that shelf at 730 mm.
    5. Two quarter-ellipse brackets, stowed behind the flap, turn 90° on steel pins and carry the desk.
    6. Two runners under the carcass reach 210 mm forward, so the open desk can't tip it. The wings sweep over them and hide them when closed.

    What is assumed

    • Sizes come from perspective measurements of the photos. The closed width read between 700 and 750 mm, so allow about ±5% on everything.
    • The tenons, grooves and the inside of the supports can't be seen in the photos. They are drawn the way a joiner would most likely make them.
    • The hinges in the photos look like knuckle hinges on the corner. Butt hinges are the simplest fit for a 160° swing; the real ones may be a heavier pivot type.
    • Only the left wing has a pull. The right wing opens by its edge once the left one is out of the way.

    Hand gestures

    • Press Hand gestures above the model and allow the camera. The hand model downloads once, about 10 MB.
    • Open palm, moving: orbit the model. Pinch thumb and index, then pull your hand towards the camera to zoom in, or away to zoom out.
    • Hold a fist for half a second to explode or reassemble. Hold a V sign to open or close the cabinet.
    • Tracking runs in your browser with MediaPipe; no video leaves your device. Mouse and touch keep working the whole time, and the camera switches off by itself after 45 seconds away from the model.
